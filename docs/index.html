Epithelial Barrier-Injury Transcriptomic Stratification Across Human IBD Mucosal Cohorts. Omics : a journal of integrative biology  |  2026-10-23

Public mucosal transcriptomic datasets provide an opportunity to examine whether experimental epithelial barrier biology is reflected in human inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) endpoints. We constructed a prespecified barrier-injury transcriptomic score from an inositol polyphosphate multikinase-histone deacetylase 3-matrix metalloproteinase (MMP) barrier-biology gene set and epithelial junctional genes, then evaluated it across 10 public IBD mucosal transcriptomic cohorts (2172 scored biopsies) and an independent prospective adult cohort (GSE193677; 1162 participants). Adult cohorts were analyzed for mucosal healing, clinical remission, or treatment response; pediatric and early onset cohorts were analyzed separately. In UNIFI (GSE206285), a higher baseline score was inversely associated with week 8 mucosal healing (odds ratio [OR]: 0.56, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.45-0.71, p < 0.001). In GSE193677, the score was associated with IBD compared with control status (OR: 1.71, 95% CI: 1.45-2.02) and with endoscopic and histologic activity. The association was directionally consistent across biopsy-selection analyses and exceeded expression- and variance-matched random-gene estimates (empirical p = 0.034), while MMP and inflammatory comparators were at least as strong. Single-cell data localized the MMP module predominantly to stromal cells. These findings support retrospective transcriptomic stratification of mucosal injury states and define a barrier biology-motivated bulk mucosal readout shaped by stromal inflammatory remodeling in human tissue.

Integrating complementary datasets for constructing microbiome-metabolome-disease pathways. Bioinformatics (Oxford, England)  |  2026-10-20

Microbiome-metabolome-disease pathways play a central role in host health, but studying them is limited by the data available. Large clinical microbiome cohorts often lack metabolomic measurements, while multi-omics studies with paired profiles are typically small or incompletely phenotyped. Combining such complementary datasets is challenging: the metabolite is not measured in the clinical cohort, microbiome data are high-dimensional and compositional, and log-ratio analyses depend on an arbitrarily chosen reference taxon. We propose a framework that integrates a large target cohort that has microbiome and outcome data with a smaller external study that has paired microbiome-metabolome profiles. A high-dimensional structural equation model jointly links the microbiome, the metabolome and the disease outcome. It allows for unmeasured confounding and groups microbes into functional categories. We address compositionality with the additive log-ratio (ALR) transformation, estimate parameters by penalized two-stage least squares, and test metabolite-disease associations with a regularized permutation test. A novel multiple-reference procedure treats each microbe in turn as the ALR reference. It combines the resulting p-values with the Cauchy combination test and classifies microbes by stability selection, which makes inference robust to the choice of reference. In simulations, the method controls the type I error rate, and its power increases with both target and external sample sizes. An application to inflammatory bowel disease, integrating the FRAN and iHMP cohorts, reveals biologically interpretable microbiome-metabolome-disease links. The method is implemented in the R package MMDP, freely available at https://github.com/lei-fang-stat/MMDP. Supplementary data are available at Bioinformatics online.

KCTD10 Modulates DSS-induced Ulcerative Colitis via Regulating Intestinal Stem Cell Function. The American journal of pathology  |  2026-10-20

The incidence of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) continues to rise annually, which severely threatens public health. While estimates indicate that current therapies cause a 30% IBD clinical treatment failure, the precise pathogenesis remains unclear. Here, we found that a key embryonic development regulator, KCTD10, is correlated with the severity of IBD. We observed enhanced KCTD10 expression during enterocolitis. Intestinal epithelial cell (IEC)-specific knockout of KCTD10 in mice impaired intestinal epithelial barrier function, activated intestinal adaptive immune response, and disrupted intestinal homeostasis. Furthermore, in a dextran sodium sulfate (DSS) -induced ulcerative colitis model, IEC deletion of KCTD10 exacerbated colonic inflammation and impaired post-injury repair. Mechanistically, KCTD10 deletion downregulated intestinal stem cell (ISC) marker genes and affected their function. Importantly, KCTD10 stabilizes the transcription factor ATOH1 by regulating its ubiquitination, thereby determining the fate of intestinal progenitor cells derived from Lgr5+ intestinal stem cells. Our data thus revealed a previously undescribed function of KCTD10 in modulating ulcerative colitis by regulating intestinal stem cell function.

Crosstalk through cholinergic and aryl hydrocarbon receptor pathways in the anti-inflammatory actions of microbial metabolites. Naunyn-Schmiedeberg’s archives of pharmacology  |  2026-10-20

The intestinal epithelium maintains immune and metabolic homeostasis at the host-microbiota interface, and its disruption contributes to inflammatory bowel disease (IBD). Microbial metabolites such as short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs) and indole derivatives are known to regulate epithelial and immune functions, but their roles in cholinergic signaling and aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR) pathways remain incompletely understood. Caco-2 intestinal epithelial cells were stimulated with lipopolysaccharide (LPS; 10 µg/mL) and co-treated with SCFAs (acetate, propionate, butyrate) or indole derivatives (IAA, IPA, IBA; 1000 µM). The AhR antagonist CH223191 was used to assess receptor involvement. Inflammatory markers (TNF-α, IL-6, COX-2), barrier integrity (FITC-dextran permeability, ZO-1 and occludin), cholinergic components (CHAT, ACHE, BCHE, α7nAChR, mAChR1, mAChR3), and AhR activation (nuclear localization, CYP1A1 expression) were analyzed using qPCR, ELISA, immunostaining, and enzymatic assays. Molecular docking was performed to predict metabolite-protein interactions. LPS induced inflammatory cytokine expression and barrier disruption, whereas SCFAs and indole derivatives significantly reduced inflammation, restored tight-junction organization, and decreased permeability. These metabolites enhanced CHAT and muscarinic receptor expression while suppressing ACHE upregulation, indicating restoration of cholinergic balance. Both groups reactivated AhR signaling, and AhR inhibition disrupted cholinergic regulation, supporting an AhR-dependent mechanism. Butyrate and indole-3-butyric acid exhibited the strongest protective effects. These findings identify a novel AhR-cholinergic axis in intestinal epithelial regulation and highlight microbial metabolites as promising therapeutic candidates for IBD.

An engineered probiotic for diagnosis and mucosal healing in inflammatory bowel disease.★ Cell reports  |  2026-10-20

Early diagnosis and timely treatment of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) are critical to limit disease progression, yet integrated diagnostic-therapeutic strategies remain limited. Here, we design a synthetic probiotic system that responds to reactive oxygen species (ROS) to enable noninvasive detection of intestinal inflammation. We further engineer EcN-IL22, a therapeutic strain that conditionally expresses interleukin 22-fragment crystallizable (IL-22-Fc) fusion protein in situ to mitigate colitis-associated tissue damage. Mechanistically, EcN-IL22 activates epithelial IL-22 receptor 1/signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (IL-22R1/STAT3) signaling, promoting epithelial barrier restoration and intestinal stem cell proliferation. We identify thyroid hormone receptor-interacting protein 13 (Trip13) and fidgetin-like 1 (Fignl1) as IL-22R1-responsive candidate genes associated with epithelial repair. Importantly, replacing the diagnostic output of the ROS-responsive circuit with IL-22-Fc enables inflammation-triggered therapeutic delivery and significantly alleviates colitis. Finally, EcN-IL22 demonstrates robust protective efficacy in a piglet colitis model, supporting its translational potential. Together, this work establishes an integrated probiotic platform for IBD diagnosis and regenerative therapy.

Gastrodin alleviates acute colitis with comorbid anxiety- and depression-like behaviours rather than cognitive dysfunction by modulating gut microbiota and suppressing inflammation. Naunyn-Schmiedeberg’s archives of pharmacology  |  2026-10-19

Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) patients frequently suffer from emotional disturbances, predominantly anxiety- and depression-like symptoms, and may also have cognitive dysfunction, but the mechanisms remain unclear, and no therapies targeting the gut or nervous system exist. Gastrodin (GAS), the primary active component of Gastrodia elata, has anti-inflammatory and neuroprotective effects. Whether GAS can alleviate acute colitis with comorbid anxiety- and depression-like behaviours or cognitive dysfunction via the gut-brain axis is unknown. Using a 3% dextran sulfate sodium (DSS)-induced mouse model, we administered GAS (240 mg/kg) by gavage for 7 days. The open field, elevated plus maze, tail suspension, and forced swimming tests showed that GAS improved behavioural abnormalities (including anxiety- and depression-like behaviours); it also restored colon length and reduced colonic histopathological injury. Metagenomic sequencing revealed that GAS reshaped the gut microbiota, increasing Sporosarcina and Nitrospirae while decreasing Clostridiaceae and Lachnospiraceae. Untargeted metabolomics indicated that GAS shifted the metabolic profile away from DSS and normalized taurine levels, and KEGG pathway analysis suggested that GAS may partially ameliorate DSS-induced metabolic disturbances by downregulating virulence-related pathways and upregulating energy metabolic pathways such as the TCA cycle and oxidative phosphorylation relative to DSS. Western blot confirmed that GAS restored the tight junction protein Occludin and decreased TNF-α. Quantitative proteomics demonstrated that GAS reversed the aberrant expression of 191 colonic proteins. These results demonstrate correlative associations between GAS treatment and the alleviation of acute colitis and behavioural abnormalities, alongside concurrent changes in gut microbiota composition, metabolite profiles, and intestinal barrier proteins. Whether these associations reflect a causal gut-brain axis mechanism requires direct experimental validation. GAS modulates the microbiota, partially restores metabolism, repairs the gut barrier, and suppresses local inflammation, suggesting a potential gut-brain axis mechanism. These findings position GAS as a promising candidate for further mechanistic and translational investigation in IBD-associated behavioural comorbidities.

Reviewing preclinical evidence for potent anti-colitogenic effects of the “bee glue” propolis.Review European journal of microbiology & immunology  |  2026-10-19

The incidence and disease burden of inflammatory bowel diseases including ulcerative colitis have risen significantly in recent years, and current treatment remains limited by adverse effects and insufficient long-term efficacy, creating demand for alternative or adjunct treatment options. The “bee glue” propolis, a natural compound with a long history of medicinal use, has attracted growing attention for its anti-oxidant, anti-inflammatory, and immune-modulatory properties. This review summarizes and critically evaluates research into anti-colitogenic effects of propolis and its bioactive molecules in experimental colitis animal models. Our structured literature search revealed 21 preclinical studies showing that application of propolis and derivatives resulted in clinical and histopathological improvement of colitis. Treated animals presented with reduced colonic inflammation, restored intestinal barrier integrity, and a more diverse commensal gut microbiota. Notably, the composition of propolis varied considerably between studies depending on geographical and botanical origins and extraction method of the compound, for instance. In conclusion, propolis and derivatives exert potent anti-colitogenic effects in vivo, and might constitute promising disease-alleviating treatment options in human intestinal inflammation, including ulcerative colitis that should be further unravelled in future clinical trials.

Monocyte surface Y RNAs potentiate innate immunity. Nature cell biology  |  2026-10-19

RNAs can associate with the surface of diverse cell types, yet the functions of specific RNA species on primary immune cells remain unclear. Here, utilizing methods developed to profile, image and quantify surface RNAs in rare primary cell populations, we found that Y RNAs, a family of small noncoding RNAs, preferentially localize to human monocyte surface. In an exploratory pooled inflammatory bowel disease cohort, the proportion of cell-surface-Y-RNA-positive peripheral monocytes was markedly higher in patients with inflammatory bowel disease than in healthy donors or individuals with irritable bowel syndrome. In vivo depletion of cell-surface Y RNAs ameliorates disease activity in mice with colitis. Mechanistically, cell-surface Y RNAs co-cluster with Toll-like receptors TLR2 and TLR4, bind extracellular histones and promote receptor-mediated histone recognition, thereby activating NF-κB and AP-1 signalling and increasing IL6 expression and IL-6 secretion. These findings identify cell-surface Y RNAs as previously unrecognized amplifiers of inflammatory activation.

Myrica rubra juice attenuates DSS-induced colitis and is associated with changes in the gut microbiota-metabolite-host axis. Food & function  |  2026-10-19

Ulcerative colitis (UC) is characterized by chronic intestinal inflammation and impaired mucosal barrier function. Myrica rubra contains polyphenols and flavonoids, but the mechanisms underlying its potential protective effects in colitis remain unclear. We evaluated Myrica rubra juice (MRJ) in a dextran sulfate sodium (DSS)-induced mouse model of colitis and a TNF-α-injured colonic organoid model. The study combined 16S rRNA gene sequencing, untargeted serum and fecal metabolomics, and molecular docking. MRJ treatment attenuated disease severity and was associated with increased expression of occludin and MUC2 in DSS-treated mice. MRJ also altered microbial diversity and taxonomic composition in a taxon-specific manner. Molecular docking generated predicted poses and docking scores for selected parent compound-protein pairs. These calculations did not demonstrate physical binding, functional inhibition, or target engagement in vivo. Together, the findings support a direct epithelial response and identify concurrent microbiota-associated changes. However, the present design could not determine whether these microbial changes contributed to protection. It also could not separate or quantify the host-directed and microbiota-mediated contributions or identify the active chemical species.

Primetime for the Gasdermins Seesaw in Intestinal Homeostasis and Digestive Disorders.Review Medicinal research reviews  |  2026-10-18

The five pore-forming gasdermins (GSDMs, namely GSDMA-E) are best known for executing pyroptosis, a lytic and highly inflammatory form of programmed cell death. Accumulating evidence over the past decade has unveiled diverse nonlytic functions of GSDMs in digestive homeostasis and disease. GSDMs preserve intestinal barrier integrity and mucosal immunity under physiological conditions, whereas their dysregulated activation fuels tissue injury across a spectrum of digestive diseases, including inflammatory bowel disease, acute intestinal injury, radiation enteritis, acute pancreatitis, and liver disorders. Notably, the functional outcome of GSDM activation is highly context-dependent: the same protein can be either protective or injurious. This duality extends to digestive oncology, where GSDM-mediated pyroptosis can stimulate or suppress antitumor immunity. GSDM-targeted drugs, including GSDMD agonists, GSDMD inhibitors, and GSDME inhibitors, have shown encouraging efficacy in preclinical models, although none have yet reached the clinic. Here, we review the dual roles of GSDMs across the digestive system, outline the mechanistic basis of their form-, cell-, and tissue-specific functions, and discuss the progress and remaining challenges in GSDM-targeted therapy.

Microbiome-Endocannabinoidome Crosstalk as an Immunometabolic Rheostat in Inflammatory Disease.Review FASEB bioAdvances  |  2026-10-15

Chronic inflammatory diseases reflect disrupted coordination among microbial ecology, epithelial barriers, lipid signaling, and immunity. The microbiome-endocannabinoidome (eCBome) axis is already recognized as a regulator of metabolism, permeability, and inflammatory tone; the unresolved issue is how to distinguish direct crosstalk from parallel or associative changes. This review therefore operationalizes the axis as an immunometabolic rheostat defined by five measurable domains: microbial functions and metabolites, compartment-specific eCBome ligands and enzymes, barrier integrity and microbial translocation, immune-cell programming, and tissue or clinical outcomes. We distinguish classical endocannabinoids (anandamide and 2-arachidonoylglycerol) from the endocannabinoid-related N-acylethanolamines palmitoylethanolamide and oleoylethanolamide, which act prominently through PPARα, TRPV1, GPR119, and related pathways. Evidence is appraised as direct causal, mechanistically supportive, associative, or hypothesis-generating. The strongest causal foundation links microbiota manipulation to intestinal eCBome tone, permeability, and metabolic endotoxemia, while disease-specific evidence is most developed in obesity-associated inflammation and inflammatory bowel disease. Engineered Lactobacillus paracasei platforms that produce PEA or OEA in situ provide particularly informative proof-of-concept because they couple a defined microbial perturbation to lipid-mediator release, receptor-dependent barrier effects, and inflammatory or metabolic outcomes. By contrast, evidence in MASLD/MASH, neuroinflammation, and systemic autoimmunity remains more compartment-dependent and heterogeneous. Clinical translation must account for the psychiatric toxicity that ended centrally active CB1 antagonism with rimonabant, the target and off-target complexity of FAAH/MAGL inhibition, and limitations of surrogate barrier biomarkers. A causal roadmap should combine gnotobiotic and loss-of-function models, isotope tracing, organoids, and gut-on-chip systems with tissue-resolved lipidomics and longitudinal, biomarker-enriched human trials.

Aucubin Alleviates Ulcerative Colitis in Mice by Repairing the Intestinal Barrier and Modulating the Microbiota-Metabolism Axis. Food science & nutrition  |  2026-10-15

Ulcerative colitis (UC) is a chronic inflammatory bowel disease with a complex pathogenesis, necessitating the development of safe and effective therapeutic interventions. This study aimed to investigate the protective effects and underlying mechanisms of Aucubin (Auc) against dextran sulfate sodium (DSS)-induced UC in mice. Using a DSS-induced colitis model, we systematically evaluated the multifaceted effects of Auc by assessing the Disease Activity Index (DAI), colon length, histopathological changes, immunohistochemical staining, ELISA results, proteomic profiles, 16S rRNA sequencing data, and untargeted metabolomic analyses. Compared with the DSS group, Aucubin high-dose (Auc-H, 100 mg/kg) treatment significantly attenuated DSS-induced body weight loss, DAI score elevation, and colon shortening (all p < 0.05). The therapeutic efficacy of Auc-H was comparable to that of the positive control drug 5-aminosalicylic acid (5-ASA). Mechanistically, Auc restored intestinal barrier integrity by stimulating mucin 2 (Muc2) secretion and upregulating the expression of zonula occludens-1 (ZO-1) and Occludin, which in turn significantly reduced serum lipopolysaccharide (LPS) concentrations (p < 0.001). Proteomic analysis identified 2180 DSS-induced differentially expressed proteins, of which 679 were significantly counter-regulated by Auc-H, mainly involving the complement and coagulation cascades and immune-inflammatory pathways. Furthermore, gut microbiota analysis showed that Auc promoted the growth of beneficial bacteria, improving microbial balance. Untargeted metabolomics further revealed that Auc-H significantly restored the levels of 120 DSS-induced differential metabolites, including 86 upregulated and 34 downregulated metabolites, among which were protective tryptophan-derived indole metabolites (p < 0.05). Consequently, Aucubin exhibits significant clinical potential in alleviating UC via a multifaceted mechanism. These effects include reduced inflammation, restored epithelial barrier integrity, and modulated microbial metabolism. These findings provide crucial experimental evidence supporting the potential application of Auc as a preventive therapy for UC.

Efficacy and Safety of Guselkumab in Patients with Crohn’s Disease and Ulcerative Colitis: A Prospective Real-World Study.★ Clinical gastroenterology and hepatology : the official clinical practice journal of the American Gastroenterological Association  |  2026-10-22

Guselkumab (GUS), selective IL-23p19 inhibitor that also binds to CD64+ cells, is approved for the treatment of moderately-to-severely active Crohn’s disease (CD) and ulcerative colitis (UC). Real-world evidence of effectiveness and safety is needed. We prospectively enrolled adult patients with IBD initiating GUS at our tertiary center between 9/2024 and 1/2026. Patients were followed for up to 52 weeks. Clinical and biochemical data were collected at designated time points on patients’ activity indices and adverse events (AEs). Clinical remission was defined as a Harvey-Bradshaw Index (HBI) <5 for CD or Simple Clinical Colitis Activity Index (SCCAI) ≤2 for UC. We followed 234 patients who initiated GUS (142 CD, 92 UC). Week 12 clinical remission was achieved in 76.8% of patients with CD (HBI<5) and 80.7% of patients with UC (SCCAI≤2). Among patients with active disease at baseline, week 12 remission rates were 24/41 (58.5%) in CD and 35/48 (72.9%) in UC. Remission was maintained at month 6 in 88.4% in CD. In UC, remission rates at 6 months were maintained in 85.4% of patients. Normal CRP at week 12 was observed in 72.9% patients with CD and 90.0% in UC. Disease location, extent, or behavior were not associated with different response rates. AEs occurred in 15 patients (6.4%) and were mostly mild; 7 patients discontinued GUS due to AEs. In this large real-world prospective experience of GUS for IBD, we describe high rates of remission and an overall safety profile that is similar to that seen in the pivotal trials.

Risankizumab improved disease outcomes among patients with moderately to severely active ulcerative colitis in East Asian countries, with a safety profile consistent with the global study.
