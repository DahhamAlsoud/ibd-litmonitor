Cadmium sulfide nanoparticles exacerbate colitis via Faecalibaculum rodentium-mediated modulation of the indole-3-lactic acid-Th17 axis. Chemico-biological interactions  |  2026-11-11

With the increasing application of cadmium sulfide nanoparticles (CdS-NPs), their potential adverse effects on intestinal health have attracted growing attention. However, the impact of CdS-NPs exposure on inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) and the underlying mechanisms remain unclear. Here, we demonstrate that daily oral gavage exposure to 1 mg/kg CdS-NPs for 4 weeks or 11 weeks alone did not induce apparent colonic inflammation in healthy mice but exacerbated both acute and chronic DSS-induced colitis, as evidenced by severe weight loss, increased disease activity, colon shortening, tissue damage, and immune infiltration. Integrated gut microbiota profiling and metabolomic analysis revealed that CdS-NPs markedly reduced the abundance and tryptophan-metabolizing capacity of Faecalibaculum rodentium (F. rodentium), leading to impaired indole-3-lactic acid (ILA) production. F. rodentium administration significantly alleviated CdS-NPs-aggravated DSS-induced colitis and restored serum ILA levels, accompanied by suppression of Th17 responses. Consistently, exogenous ILA treatment attenuated intestinal inflammation and suppressed Th17/IL-17 responses in vivo and inhibited Th17 differentiation in CD4+ T cells in vitro. Collectively, our findings identify microbial tryptophan metabolism and IL-17 signaling as critical mediators of intestinal inflammatory sensitization associated with CdS-NPs exposure and highlight the F. rodentium-ILA axis as a potential intervention target for CdS-NPs-induced intestinal inflammation.

Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) stems from gut dysbiosis, impaired intestinal epithelial barriers and persistent mucosal immune dysfunction. Free oral probiotics or probiotic-derived extracellular vesicles yield poor therapeutic effects against IBD, as they degrade rapidly in the gastrointestinal tract and lack specific tropism for inflamed colon lesions. To overcome these delivery limitations, we fabricated pH-responsive hyaluronic acid (HA)-decorated alginate microcapsules (ALHA@LGG&EBMVs) to co-deliver viable Lactobacillus rhamnosus GG (LGG) and extrusion-derived bacterial membrane vesicles (EBMVs). The electrosprayed Ca2+-crosslinked alginate skeleton confers robust gastric-acid protection, and surface-immobilized HA enables specific binding to CD44 overexpressed on inflamed colon epithelium. In vitro simulated gastric fluid assays revealed that the ALHA shell afforded superior protection to encapsulated LGG and EBMVs. In vivo fluorescence tracing on dextran sulfate sodium-induced colitic mice showed ALHA@LGG&EBMVs achieved 2.7-fold higher cargo accumulation at inflammatory colon sites with 24 h prolonged lesion retention relative to untargeted cargo-loaded alginate microcapsules (AL@LGG&EBMVs). In vivo therapeutic assays verified that the dual-cargo microcapsules mitigated colitis symptoms, restored epithelial tight junctions and reduced excessive intestinal epithelial permeability. Mechanistic studies revealed concurrent improvements across three key pathological dimensions: balancing the abnormal Th17/Treg ratio, promoting anti-inflammatory M2 macrophage polarization, and remodeling dysbiotic gut microbiota to expand beneficial commensals and reduce the abundance of pro-inflammatory pathobionts, which collectively facilitate the restoration of intestinal homeostasis. This polysaccharide delivery system integrates gastric-acid shielding and active inflammatory-lesion targeting to achieve multi-dimensional restoration of intestinal homeostasis. This proof-of-concept study offers a biocompatible microecological nanomedicine prototype for IBD intervention and establishes a blueprint for the development of oral gut-targeted biomaterials.

Dsg2 truncation causes a severe intestinal epithelial barrier breakdown and alters microbiome and immune response. JCI insight  |  2026-11-10

Inflammatory bowel diseases (IBD) including Crohn’s disease (CD) are characterized by impaired intestinal epithelial barrier (IEB) function. Alterations in the desmosomal cadherin desmoglein 2 (Dsg2) have been linked to loss of IEB and intestinal inflammation in IBD. We investigated the pathogenic impact of a cytoplasmatic-truncating mutation in the desmosomal cadherin desmoglein 2 gene (DSG2) in an IBD patient. Genetic analysis of a CD patient revealed a likely pathogenic DSG2 mutation leading to a truncated protein lacking part of the intracellular domain. Generation of an enterocyte-specific mouse model to recapitulate the human mutation resulted in a lethal intestinal barrier defect which was evident in homozygous but not in heterozygous mice. In vitro, barrier defects correlated with changes in Dsg2 mobility and upregulation of pore-forming tight junction protein claudin-2. Organoids derived from heterozygous Dsg2 truncated mice exhibited increased epithelial susceptibility to proinflammatory cytokines. Bulk RNAseq followed by gene ontology analysis in homozygous mice revealed a skewed IL17/IL23 response overlapping with transcriptional changes in patients with CD. Furthermore, 16S rRNA sequencing revealed alterations in microbiome composition in animals that carried the Dsg2 truncation. In summary, a patient-derived mutation in the DSG2 gene causing cytoplasmic truncation in homozygous state induced a lethal intestinal barrier breakdown in mice paralleled by altered gut microbiota and intestinal immune response.

An emerging focus in microbiome science is on changes to people’s microbiomes following immigration, particularly to so-called Western countries, such as the USA, Canada, and Australia. Moving to these regions has been linked to microbiome changes that may increase immigrants’ susceptibility to health problems, including obesity and Inflammatory Bowel Disease. The term “Westernization” (and adjacent terminology) is frequently used to describe changes in diet, lifestyle, and built environment - and the consequent microbiome shifts - associated with immigration. However, what Westernization means in this context, and whether it is an appropriate descriptor, is often unclear. The purpose of this article is to provide a review of the diverse meanings associated with the terms “West”, “Western”, “Westernization” (and similar) across microbiome-related research and broader uses. We argue that these terms can obscure the specific structural, environmental, and cultural factors shaping microbiome changes. We discuss the consequences of this imprecision and provide recommendations for future research. Avoiding oversimplified conclusions about what impacts immigrants’ microbiome and health can contribute to more accurate targets for public health interventions for these groups in the long term.

Ulcerative colitis (UC) is a chronic inflammatory bowel disease for which current therapies remain inadequate. Caffeic acid (CA), a natural hydroxycinnamic acid polyphenol, possesses anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties. However, whether CA engages the gut microbiota-tryptophan-aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AHR)-Nrf2 axis in UC has not been established. To evaluate the anti-UC activity of CA in vivo and in vitro and to elucidate its underlying mechanisms through a multi-platform integrative strategy. A dextran sulfate sodium (DSS)-induced murine colitis model and lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-stimulated mouse colonic epithelial cells (MCECs) were used in parallel. Mechanism was addressed by combining microbiota, metabolomic, transcriptomic and proteomic profiling with faecal microbiota transplantation, computational and biophysical target validation, and siRNA-mediated AHR knockdown. CA was administered at 10 and 20 mg/kg/day, with 5-aminosalicylic acid as positive control. Complementary platforms included 16S rRNA gut microbiota sequencing, untargeted serum metabolomics, colonic transcriptomic profiling, data-independent acquisition mass spectrometry proteomics, human UC single-cell RNA sequencing (GEO: GSE214695), faecal microbiota transplantation into antibiotic-pretreated recipients, molecular docking, 100 ns molecular dynamics simulation, cellular thermal shift assay, dual-luciferase reporter assay, and co-immunoprecipitation. CA dose-dependently alleviated dextran sulfate sodium-induced colitis, suppressed NF-κB activation, and reduced interleukin-6, interleukin-1β, and tumour necrosis factor alpha. Gut microbiota profiling revealed enrichment of Bifidobacterium and Dubosiella with depletion of Escherichia-Shigella. Transplantation of the CA-remodelled microbiota into antibiotic-pretreated recipients reproduced the protective phenotype and restored colonic AHR and cytochrome P450 1A1 expression, demonstrating that the remodelled community is sufficient to confer protection. CA ameliorates UC through convergent AHR activation, arising from both microbiota-mediated tryptophan reprogramming and direct receptor binding, with AHR-dependent Nrf2/NQO1/HO-1 induction and NF-κB suppression to restore intestinal barrier integrity. These findings support CA as a multi-target phytochemical candidate for UC treatment.

Th17 suppression strategies: targeting IL-17 and RORγt pathways in autoimmune diseases.Review Frontiers in immunology  |  2026-10-27

T helper 17 (Th17) cells and their key effector cytokine, particularly IL-17A and IL-17F, play central roles in host defense against extracellular pathogens. However, they are also critically involved in the pathogenesis of many autoimmune and inflammatory diseases. Th17 cell differentiation, stabilization, plasticity, and pathogenicity are regulated by a network of cytokines and transcription factors, including IL-6, TGF-β, IL-23, STAT3, and the key transcription factor RORγt. RORγt-dependent pathways are also relevant to other immune populations, including group 3 innate lymphoid cells (ILC3s), highlighting the complexity of the IL-17 axis across tissues. Dysregulation of these pathways can lead to chronic inflammation and tissue damage in diseases such as psoriasis, rheumatoid arthritis, multiple sclerosis, and inflammatory bowel disease. Therapeutic targeting of the Th17 axis has led to clinically established approaches, particularly direct blockade of IL-17 signaling and inhibition of IL-23, whereas direct RORγt inhibition remains an investigational strategy. RORγt inhibitors may modulate Th17 differentiation and broader RORγt-dependent transcriptional programs, but their clinical development has been challenged by variable efficacy, safety considerations, and translational limitations. This review provides a comprehensive overview of Th17 biology, focusing on differentiation, plasticity, tissue-specific functions, and the contribution of Th17 and other RORγt-expression populations to autoimmune diseases. Furthermore, it critically compares established IL-17-targeted therapies with emerging RORγt inhibitors, emphasizing their distinct mechanisms, cellular and molecular targets, clinical evidence, safety, and developmental status. Finally, we discuss combination strategies, translational challenges, and emerging biomarkers that may contribute to more individualized therapeutic approaches and patient stratification.

They primarily include acetate, propionate, and butyrate. In recent years, a large body of research has revealed that SCFAs are not only an important energy source for colonic epithelial cells but also key signaling molecules linking the gut microbiota to the host immune system. SCFAs play a central role in immune regulation. This review summarizes the production and characteristics of SCFAs. In addition, this article summarizes treatment strategies targeting SCFAs, including dietary interventions, probiotics, prebiotics, and fecal microbiota transplantation. It also discusses the current challenges in this field and outlines future directions, with the aim of providing new insights for the prevention and treatment of immune-related diseases.

Safety is important when selecting advanced therapies for inflammatory bowel disease (IBD). Safety data for anti-interleukin therapy (anti-IL) or vedolizumab come from trials with limited follow-up, and direct comparisons with anti-tumor necrosis factors (anti-TNFs) for incident cancer are lacking. We evaluated cancer incidence in patients with IBD treated with anti-IL therapy or vedolizumab versus anti-TNFs. We conducted a retrospective cohort study of adults with IBD and no prior cancer who initiated anti-IL therapy, vedolizumab or anti-TNFs. Our primary outcome was incident cancer from 90 days after therapy initiation through discontinuation. We calculated incidence rates and performed Cox proportional hazards models. We identified 4,019 advanced therapy starts: 995 anti-IL, 692 vedolizumab, and 2,332 anti-TNF starts, with mean therapy duration of 1096 days. In multivariable analysis, there was a trend towards a lower hazard of all cancers among anti-IL users compared to anti-TNF users (HR 0.47; 95% CI 0.21-1.04), which was significant on sensitivity analyses. We found no evidence of an increased cancer risk with anti-IL therapy compared with anti-TNF therapy in patients with IBD. There was no difference in the cancer risk between vedolizumab and anti-TNF therapy.
