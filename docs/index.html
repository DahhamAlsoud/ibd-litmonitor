Peripheral T- and B-cell immune signatures associated with response to vedolizumab in ulcerative colitis.★ Inflammatory bowel diseases  |  2026-11-06

Vedolizumab (VDZ) blocks integrin α4β7-MAdCAM-1 interactions, limiting leukocyte entry into the gut, yet peripheral-blood immune correlates of clinical response in ulcerative colitis (UC) remain unidentified. Peripheral blood mononuclear cells from healthy controls (HCs) and patients with UC, stratified by VDZ exposure and clinical response, were analyzed using mass cytometry (cytometry by time-of-flight). A 38-marker panel enriched for integrins, chemokine receptors, and activation markers was applied. Major immune populations were interrogated using lineage-specific opt-SNE and FlowSOM clustering. The dataset included 26 HCs, 30 VDZ-naive UC (Nancy index ≥ 2), 8 VDZ-treated non-responders (Nancy index ≥ 2), and 8 VDZ-treated responders (Nancy index <2). No significant differences were detected in myeloid, NK-cell, or TCRγδ+ T-cell compartments. Integrin α4β7+ cells were detected across multiple immune lineages and were enriched in the B-cell compartment. Between-group differences were observed in the CD4+ T-cell and B-cell compartments. VDZ responders showed enrichment of an α4β7+ effector memory CD4+ T-cell subset compared with VDZ-naive UC. In parallel, responders exhibited reduced frequencies of class-switched plasmablast and activated B-cell populations relative to VDZ-naive UC. Integrin-pairing analysis demonstrated that these B-cell populations contained both α4β7+ and α4β1+ cells. In contrast, VDZ non-responders showed enrichment of an α4β7+ atypical-like IgA+ memory B-cell subset relative to responders, VDZ-naive UC, and HCs. Clinical response and non-response to VDZ in UC are associated with distinct circulating T- and B-cell immune phenotypes. Peripheral-blood immunophenotyping may identify correlates of response and non-response. Prospective studies are needed to determine whether these signatures are predictive before VDZ initiation or reflect pharmacodynamic changes after treatment.

Efficacy and safety of ustekinumab and vedolizumab in very early-onset inflammatory bowel disease: A multicenter study from the pediatric inflammatory bowel disease Porto Group of ESPGHAN. Journal of pediatric gastroenterology and nutrition  |  2026-11-05

We aimed to assess vedolizumab and ustekinumab efficacy and safety in patients with very-early onset inflammatory bowel disease (VEO-IBD). A retrospective study involving 29 European and North American sites affiliated with the IBD Porto Group of European Society for Paediatric Gastroenterology Hepatology and Nutrition. Clinical and laboratory data were collected from patients with VEO-IBD who commenced vedolizumab or ustekinumab before 6 years of age, between 2014 and 2024. Ninety-six patients (60.4% females) were included (58 ulcerative colitis, 29 Crohn’s disease, and 9 IBD-Unclassified). Median (Q1-Q3) age at diagnosis was 2.3 (0.4-4.2) years, 37 (38.5%) patients were diagnosed at age <2 years old. Thirty-seven (38.5%) patients received ustekinumab, 67 (69.7%) vedolizumab, and 11 (11.5%) both drugs. Median (Q1-Q3) follow-up time was 11 (6-26) and 14 (7-39) months for ustekinumab and vedolizumab, respectively. Eighty (84.2%) patients were previously treated with infliximab, 23 (24.2%) with adalimumab, and 20 (20.8%) with both. The median (Q1-Q3) time from diagnosis to vedolizumab and ustekinumab initiation was 15 (9-26) and 20 (11-36) months, respectively. Ustekinumab-treated patients achieved clinical remission in 24.3%, 51.4%, and 40.5% at end of induction, week 26, and week 52, with steroid-free remission in 18.9%, 45.9%, and 35.3%, respectively. Vedolizumab-treated patients achieved clinical remission in 29%, 47.5%, and 40.3%, with steroid-free clinical remission (SFCR) rates of 10.4%, 43.3%, and 37.3% at the same time points. Infectious adverse events were reported in 3 (8.3%) of ustekinumab-treated and 5 (7.6%) vedolizumab-treated patients, none severe. Ustekinumab and vedolizumab demonstrated high therapeutic durability in patients with VEO-IBD; 1-year rates of SFCR were modest.

Effects of upadacitinib on lipid profiles and their relationship with inflammation in patients with inflammatory bowel disease: a real-world longitudinal cohort study.★ Inflammatory bowel diseases  |  2026-11-05

Upadacitinib (UPA), a selective Janus kinase 1 inhibitor, is approved for moderate-to-severe inflammatory bowel disease (IBD). JAK inhibitors affect lipid metabolism, but longitudinal real-world data on lipid changes during UPA therapy, and their relation to inflammatory activity, are scarce. In this single-center retrospective cohort study, patients with ulcerative colitis or Crohn’s disease treated with upadacitinib were followed for up to 2 years. Serial fasting lipid profiles and inflammatory markers (fecal calprotectin, C-reactive protein [CRP], high-sensitivity CRP) were collected at baseline, end of induction (weeks 8-12), 1 year, and 2 years. Lipid trajectories were analyzed with linear mixed-effects models; paired changes were compared with paired t-tests. Among 159 patients (mean age 43.3 ± 14.0 years; 62.3% male), total cholesterol (TC) rose from 4.19 ± 0.87 mmol/L at baseline to 4.92, 5.03, and 5.21 mmol/L at the end of induction, 1 year, and 2 years (mixed-effects estimates +0.70 to +0.90; all P < .001). Low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) and high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) rose in parallel, and the LDL-C/HDL-C ratio did not increase. CRP reductions correlated with lipid rises (Spearman rho = -0.45 to -0.47; all P ≤ .02). No cardiovascular death, myocardial infarction, or stroke occurred (0/159); one venous thromboembolism and 3 hypertension cases were recorded. In patients with IBD treated with UPA, TC and LDL-C rose modestly alongside the resolution of inflammation; HDL-C improved and the LDL-C/HDL-C ratio did not increase. Lipid monitoring and individualized cardiovascular risk assessment are warranted during UPA therapy.

A Real-World Population Pharmacokinetic Model of Adalimumab in Inflammatory Bowel Disease to Support Model-Informed Precision Dosing in Routine Clinical Practice. European journal of drug metabolism and pharmacokinetics  |  2026-11-05

Translating population pharmacokinetic (popPK) models of adalimumab in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) into clinical practice remains challenging. We aimed to develop a parsimonious popPK model using real-world therapeutic drug monitoring data and perform dosing simulations to guide model-informed precision dosing (MIPD). We conducted a retrospective, longitudinal study of adult IBD patients treated with adalimumab between 2022 and 2024. Serum adalimumab concentrations from routine therapeutic drug monitoring were analysed using nonlinear mixed-effects modelling. Covariates were selected using a stepwise likelihood ratio approach. Model performance was evaluated using goodness-of-fit (GOF) plots, prediction-corrected visual predictive checks (pcVPC), normalised prediction distribution errors (NPDE), sequential Bayesian forecasting analysis, and bootstrap analysis. Monte Carlo simulations estimated the probability of target attainment (PTA) for steady-state trough concentration thresholds of ≥ 8, ≥ 10, and ≥ 12 mg·l⁻1 across different body weight, serum albumin, and dosing scenarios. From 201 patients (56.2% male; 48.6 (±16.2) years), 709 trough concentrations were analysed. A one-compartment model with first-order elimination and absorption (fixed at 0.151·day⁻1) adequately described the pharmacokinetics. Population estimates were apparent clearance (CL/F) 0.288 l·day⁻1 and apparent volume of distribution 5.0 l. Body weight and serum albumin were significant covariates on CL/F, reducing interindividual variability from 59.2% to 42.0%. Model performance was consistent across evaluation methods. Individual parameter estimation was reliable with low CL/F shrinkage (11.1%). Predictive performance was robust: minimal bias (mean prediction error [MPE]: -0.30 mg·l⁻1) and high precision (root mean square error [RMSE]: 3.86 mg·l⁻1). pcVPC demonstrated good agreement (91.0% coverage), and NPDE indicated no structural misspecifications. Sequential forecasting analysis showed that predictive performance improved as more data were incorporated (MPE decreased from 0.37 to 0.03 mg·l⁻1), and precision increased (RMSE decreased from 4.86 to 4.39 mg·l⁻1). Under standard maintenance dosing, PTA ranged from 65.0% to 98.5%, from 36.5% to 93.5%, and from 20.5% to 86.5% for ≥ 8 mg·l⁻1, ≥ 10 mg·l⁻1, and ≥12 mg·l⁻1 respectively. PTA decreased with increasing body weight, lower serum albumin, and more stringent concentration thresholds. This popPK model adequately characterizes adalimumab pharmacokinetics in IBD patients. Its robust predictive performance, which progressively improves as more individual data are incorporated, together with the model-based simulations, supports its potential utility in MIPD, particularly in patients with higher clearance or when higher concentration thresholds are targeted.

Performance of an infliximab precision-guided dosing tool from a capillary blood-based self-collection device in inflammatory bowel disease patients.★ Inflammatory bowel diseases  |  2026-11-05

Infliximab (IFX) trough concentrations are routinely measured to guide dosing and optimize efficacy. Bayesian forecasting tools may enable earlier prediction of trough concentrations, while patient-friendly capillary self-collection devices could improve access to precision-guided dosing (PGD). We evaluated the performance of the PredictrPK IFX platform in forecasting IFX trough concentrations by using serum collected with an upper-arm self-collection device and compared its performance with standard venous blood obtained by phlebotomy. Patients with inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) receiving IFX maintenance therapy were recruited from a single IBD center. Midcycle samples collected via an upper-arm self-collection device and via traditional venipuncture were compared. There was also a strong association between predicted IFX concentrations and measured IFX trough concentrations. PredictrPK accurately predicted IFX trough concentrations. Capillary self-collection using an upper-arm device yielded results comparable to those from venous blood measurement, which may facilitate increased implementation and accessibility of PGD in clinical practice.

Microbial metabolism of methotrexate produces a STAT3 signaling molecule that alleviates gut inflammation. Cell host & microbe  |  2026-11-05

Methotrexate (MTX) is a gold standard therapy for inflammatory diseases but has mixed therapeutic success in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD). The gut microbiota impact MTX efficacy and toxicity by metabolizing MTX and altering its bioavailability, yet how inflammation alters this metabolism, and how the resulting metabolites act on the host, remain poorly understood. Here, we find that gut microbes, notably Clostridium asparagiforme, metabolize MTX via carboxypeptidase G2 (CPDG2) to produce deoxyaminopteroic acid (DAMPA). In intestinal epithelial cells, DAMPA preserves mitochondrial health through mitochondrial signal transducer and activator of transcription 3 (STAT3) signaling and enhances mitophagy. DAMPA administration attenuates intestinal inflammation in vivo, and remodels colonic mitochondrial pathways. IBD mouse models show reduced microbial CPDG2 and DAMPA, while patients with IBD show reduced CPDG2 gene abundance and suppressed DAMPA-enriched mitochondrial pathways, potentially explaining MTX outcomes in IBD. Together, these findings reframe microbial MTX metabolism from passive drug detoxification to active regulation of host mitochondrial and inflammatory homeostasis.

Effects of low-dose atorvastatin combined with reduced-dose prednisolone in acetic acid-induced ulcerative colitis in rats. Immunologic research  |  2026-11-05

Long-term corticosteroid treatment for ulcerative colitis (UC) is often limited by dose-dependent adverse effects, highlighting the need for strategies that reduce steroid exposure. This study evaluated the efficacy of a combination of half-doses of Atorvastatin and Prednisolone in rats with acetic acid-induced colitis. Thirty-five rats were allocated to five groups (n = 7 per group): normal control, vehicle-treated UC control, UC treated with atorvastatin (10 mg/kg/day, orally), UC treated with prednisolone (10 mg/kg/day, orally), and UC treated with the combination of atorvastatin (5 mg/kg/day) plus prednisolone (5 mg/kg/day), orally. Treatment continued for 10 days. Disease activity index (DAI), colonic edema index, histopathological leukocyte infiltration, pro-inflammatory cytokines, myeloperoxidase (MPO), malondialdehyde (MDA), nitric oxide (NO), and tissue levels of Nrf2, HO-1, and NF-kB were evaluated. Compared with untreated UC, all treatment groups showed attenuation of disease-associated changes. The half-dose combination was associated with the lowest cumulative DAI and day-10 DAI, markedly reduced leukocyte infiltration, and lower MPO and inflammatory cytokine levels than either monotherapy in several comparisons. It also normalized MDA and brought NF-kB levels toward those observed in healthy controls, while increasing Nrf2 and HO-1 levels. These findings indicate that low-dose atorvastatin combined with reduced-dose prednisolone can attenuate experimental colonic injury and inflammation more strongly than either monotherapy under the conditions tested.

Long-Term Follow-up of Azathioprine-Induced Portosinusoidal Vascular Disease in a Patient With Crohn’s Disease.Case report ACG case reports journal  |  2026-11-04

Azathioprine is widely used to treat inflammatory bowel disease, organ transplantation, and autoimmune disorders. Hepatotoxicity is a rare but serious complication. We report a 48-year-old man with ileocolonic Crohn’s disease who developed azathioprine-induced portosinusoidal vascular disease (PSVD) after 15 years of treatment. Diagnosis was suspected due to persistent cytopenia (leukopenia and thrombocytopenia) and when transabdominal ultrasonography and upper gastrointestinal endoscopy showed signs of portal hypertension. There was also an extensive portal thrombosis in the right portal vein, portal bifurcation, and origin of the left portal vein. Liver biopsy confirmed PSVD, showing nodular regenerative hyperplasia, sinusoidal dilation, and obliterative portal venopathy without cirrhosis. Treatment included azathioprine discontinuation, carvedilol, and anticoagulants. After 4 years of follow-up, Crohn’s disease remained in deep remission, portal thrombosis recanalized, and portal hypertension stabilized. This case highlights the importance of monitoring for PSVD in long-term thiopurine users.

Early decline in anti-integrin αvβ6 autoantibodies strongly predicts 1-year clinical remission in vedolizumab-treated patients with ulcerative colitis. eGastroenterology  |  2026-11-02

Anti-integrin αvβ6 immunoglobulin G autoantibodies have emerged as one of the most accurate non-invasive biomarkers for ulcerative colitis (UC). We aimed to validate their diagnostic performance in a French multicentre cohort of patients with inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) and to evaluate their predictive value for vedolizumab clinical response. We performed a multicentre, non-interventional post hoc analysis using prospectively collected serum samples and clinical data from two parent prospective observational studies: VEDOPREDIRESP (patients with IBD initiating vedolizumab) and GOLILOR (patients with UC undergoing golimumab dose intensification). Serum anti-αvβ6 autoantibody titres were quantified by ELISA at baseline and week 6 (VEDOPREDIRESP) or week 8 (GOLILOR), blinded to clinical outcomes. The primary endpoint was sustained clinical remission at week 52. In the VEDOPREDIRESP cohort (n=115; 67 UC, 48 Crohn’s disease (CD), sustained clinical remission at week 52 was achieved in 54% of patients with UC. Baseline serum anti-αvβ6 autoantibody titres were significantly higher in UC than in CD (median 271 ng/mL (IQR 38-884) vs 5.2 ng/mL (2.5-10), p<0.001), with seropositivity (>9.1 ng/mL) in 94% vs 19%, respectively. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis confirmed excellent diagnostic performance to discriminate between UC and CD (area under the ROC curve (AUC) 0.93; optimal cut-off 15 ng/mL; sensitivity 92%, specificity 95%). In patients with UC treated with vedolizumab, titres declined significantly from baseline to week 6 (median 243 ng/mL (176-884) to 190 ng/mL (89-320), p=0.040). A >30% reduction at week 6 strongly predicted sustained remission at week 52 (AUC 0.79; sensitivity 78%, specificity 80%). Multivariable analysis identified this early decline as the only statistically significant independent predictor of 1-year remission (adjusted OR 5.36, 95% CI 1.12 to 8.54, p=0.040). Notably, in an independent cohort of 47 patients with UC undergoing golimumab dose intensification, there was no reduction in median serum anti-αvβ6 autoantibody levels, with levels remaining stable from 197 ng/mL at week 0 to 205 ng/mL (97-457) at week 8 (p=0.730). Anti-αvβ6 autoantibodies robustly discriminate UC from CD. In patients with UC treated with vedolizumab, their early and selective decline predicts clinical remission at 1 year and provides a precision tool to optimise gut-selective therapy in real time.

Shorter infliximab infusion times without premedication do not increase adverse reactions. Journal of pediatric gastroenterology and nutrition  |  2026-11-02

Infliximab (IFX) infusions for paediatric inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) are resource intensive and time-intrusive on patient’s lives. Longer infusion times and administering routine pre-medications compound such timelines, despite limited supporting evidence. This study investigated whether discontinuing routine premedication and shortening IFX infusion durations increased infusion reaction rates. This retrospective comparative cohort study analysed data from 1083 IFX infusions (369 patients) at a single national referral centre. It compared a cohort receiving routine dual hydrocortisone/chlorphenamine premedication with longer infusion times (2.5 h induction/90 min maintenance; Group A) with a subsequent cohort without universal premedication and shorter infusions (2 h induction/60 min maintenance; Group B). The primary outcome was the rate of acute infusion reactions; secondary outcomes included anti-IFX antibody formation and rates of post-infusion reactions. There were 346 infusions in Group A and 737 infusions in Group B. Shorter infusions and omitting routine premedication did not increase acute infusion reaction rates (Group A: 1.5/100 vs. Group B: 1.1/100 infusions, p = 0.6; odds ratio 0.75 [0.24, 2.3]). Universal premedication neither reduced early anti-IFX antibody positivity by first maintenance dose (n = 51; 33% vs. 17%, p = 0.23) nor overall antibody prevalence (31% vs. 37%, p = 0.164). No reactions occurred during extended post-infusion observation periods in either group. Neither longer infusion times, universal pre-medication nor prolonged post-infusion observation significantly enhanced patient safety or reduced anti-drug antibody formation. Practice modification could reduce treatment burden for patients and families and improve healthcare efficiency.

A cross-sectional study on pharmacotherapy and predictors of clinical remission in patients with inflammatory bowel disease. Indian journal of pharmacology  |  2026-11-02

Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) is a chronic, progressive, and disabling disorder that includes ulcerative colitis and Crohn’s disease, characterized by periods of remission and relapse. Pharmacological management is the primary treatment option with the goal to induce mucosal healing and reduce relapse rates. The main objective of the present study was to determine the pattern of pharmacotherapy and to evaluate the factors that predict clinical remission in patients with IBD. A cross-sectional analysis was conducted involving fifty participants diagnosed with IBD. All the participants met predefined study inclusion criteria and voluntarily provided written informed consent. Data encompassing patient demographics, clinical parameters, prescribed medications, and ADRs were collected. The disease activity was evaluated using Simple Clinical Colitis Activity Index/Harvey-Bradshaw Index score. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analysis was done to assess predictors of clinical remission. The commonly prescribed drugs were mesalamine in 72%, steroids in 50%, and azathioprine in 46% patients. 30% patients had an ADR to the prescribed drug; the most common ADR was anemia and pancytopenia with azathioprine use. 52% patients were in clinical remission. Univariate analysis identified medication adherence as a significant predictor, which was confirmed on multivariate analysis. Patient’s adherent to medication were 32 times more likely to be in remission compared to nonadherent patients. Mesalamine and azathioprine were the most commonly prescribed monotherapy drugs. The most frequently prescribed combination therapies were mesalamine in conjunction with either steroids or azathioprine. Adherence to medication was the only factor statistically significantly linked to achieving clinical remission of the disease.

S1P receptors in ulcerative colitis: spotlight on etrasimod.Review Expert opinion on pharmacotherapy  |  2026-11-01

Ulcerative colitis (UC) is a chronic inflammatory bowel disease with a rising global incidence. Small molecules have transformed the treatment of UC. Specifically, sphingosine‑1-phosphate (S1P) receptor modulators are a newly available and expanding therapeutic option for patients with UC. This review analyzes the biology, efficacy, and safety of S1P receptor modulators. A literature search in PubMed regarding clinical studies published up to 1 June 2026, involving S1P receptor modulators in UC was analyzed. Clinical trial data and real-world evidence for approved S1P receptor modulators (ozanimod and etrasimod) and investigational agents (tamuzimod, mocravimod, and amiselimod) in UC treatment were gathered. Ozanimod and etrasimod both demonstrated superior efficacy over placebo in moderately to severely active UC. Both agents showed greater efficacy in biologic-naïve patients compared with biologic-exposed populations. Meta-analyses favored S1P receptor modulators over other UC therapies, particularly for inducing histologic improvement. Commonly reported adverse effects of S1P receptor modulators include elevated liver enzymes and transient asymptomatic bradycardia. Serious adverse events, including macular edema and severe infections, are rare, with infection rates comparable to those of placebo in the clinical trials. S1P receptor modulators represent a new and promising oral class of treatment for UC, showing effective results with a manageable safety profile. They are especially beneficial for patients with moderate disease severity and serve as an important option for those transitioning from traditional to advanced therapies. Although these agents have some unique safety concerns, most are mild and reversible.

Forced degradation study of etrasimod: Structural elucidation and transformation pathways of its major degradation products. Journal of pharmaceutical sciences  |  2026-11-01

Etrasimod is a sphingosine-1-phosphate receptor 1 (S1P1) receptor modulator and has been used for the treatment of moderate to severe ulcerative colitis. Understanding its chemical degradation behaviors is important for evaluation of intrinsic stability and degradation impurities. Herein, the degradation chemistry of solid etrasimod was systematically investigated under ICH-recommended stress conditions. Eight degradation products were detected by using reversed-phase HPLC, and three major products (DP-6, DP-4, and DP-2) was isolated and structurally elucidated by LC-MS combined with multidimensional NMR spectroscopy. DP-6 was assigned as an oxygenated lactone-containing degradation product, DP-4 as a ring-expanded dicarbonyl product, and DP-2 as an open-chain succinic acid derivative. Time-dependent transformation experiments demonstrated the conversion of DP-6 to DP-4 under oxidative conditions and DP-4 to DP-2 under alkaline conditions. These experimentally established transformation relationships, together with the structural information obtained for the major degradation products, support an interconnected degradation pathways involving oxidative transformation and subsequent alkaline hydrolysis. Overall, this study provides experimentally supported insights into the degradation chemistry and transformation relationships of etrasimod, which may facilitate the identification and monitoring of degradation impurities, the assessment of drug-substance stability, and the development of appropriate impurity control strategies during pharmaceutical development.
