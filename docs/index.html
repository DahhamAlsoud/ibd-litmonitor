Mechanisms and therapeutic perspectives of mucosal glycosylation-innate immunity crosstalk in inflammatory bowel disease (Review).Review International journal of molecular medicine  |  2026-10-30

Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) is a group of chronic, relapsing inflammatory disorders of the gastrointestinal tract, driven by a complex interplay of genetic susceptibility, environmental exposures, intestinal microbiota dysbiosis and immune dysregulation. In recent years, mucosal glycosylation has emerged as a fundamental molecular determinant of intestinal barrier integrity and function and is increasingly recognized as a key regulatory interface linking the microbiota with host immune responses. Concurrently, the innate immune system serves an important role in mucosal defense and inflammatory responses through pattern recognition receptor‑mediated signaling, and its aberrant activation represents a key driver of persistent inflammation in IBD. The present review systematically summarizes the bidirectional regulatory crosstalk between mucosal glycosylation and innate immunity. The mechanistic roles of glycosylation modifications are highlighted in pathogen recognition, inflammatory signal transduction, inflammasome activation and maintenance of mucosal barrier integrity. Conversely, it is discussed how innate immune activation regulates glycan biosynthesis and remodeling through inflammatory cytokines, oxidative stress and major signaling pathways, thereby reshaping the mucosal glycome in disease contexts. On this basis, the dynamic alterations of glycosylation‑innate immunity interactions across different stages of IBD, including disease initiation, progression and relapse, are delineated, emphasizing their important role in sustaining chronic inflammation and driving tissue remodeling. In addition, emerging therapeutic strategies targeting glycosylation pathways, glycan‑lectin interactions, mucosal barrier restoration and microbiota modulation are summarized. The potential of glycomics, multi‑omics integration and glyco‑immune biomarkers in disease stratification and precision medicine are also discussed. By integrating perspectives from glycobiology and immunology, the present review aims to provide a conceptual framework and novel directions for mechanistic research and targeted therapy in IBD.

Pathobiont-directed mucosal immunotherapy targets Ruminococcus gnavus and remodels host-microbiota interactions in colitis.★ Gut microbes  |  2026-10-30

Disease-associated pathobionts are recurrently enriched in inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), yet their causal contribution and selective therapeutic control remain incompletely defined. Here, through clinical microbiome profiling and causal animal studies, we identified Ruminococcus gnavus (Rg) as a key ulcerative colitis (UC)-associated pathobiont whose abundance positively correlates with intestinal oxidative stress and inflammatory levels. To selectively constrain Rg, we engineered CP-EV@Ca, an oral microbiota-directed mucosal immunotherapy that combines pathogen-specific immune targeting with redox modulation. CP-EV@Ca enhanced mucosal antigen presentation, promoted T follicular helper cell-germinal-center B-cell responses and induced robust Rg-specific secretory IgA (sIgA). CP-EV@Ca-induced mucosal immune responses, including enhanced Rg-specific sIgA production, were associated with increased immune coating and reduced Rg expansion. In both acute and chronic colitis models, CP-EV@Ca outperformed 5-aminosalicylic acid (5-ASA), suppressing inflammation and restoring microbial, epithelial and immune homeostasis. These findings demonstrate that pathobiont-specific mucosal immunity can be harnessed to selectively constrain disease-associated bacteria and restore intestinal homeostasis.

Plant-derived exosome-like nanoparticles as candidate nano-prebiotics: from structure-enabled delivery to host-microbiota interface remodeling.Review Critical reviews in food science and nutrition  |  2026-10-29

Gut ecosystem homeostasis depends on coordinated interactions among commensal microorganisms, the mucosal barrier, and the immune system. Conventional prebiotics rely on selective utilization by host microorganisms, but their efficacy is shaped by baseline microbiota and disease-associated ecological constraints. Plant-derived exosome-like nanoparticles (PELNs) are naturally occurring membrane-bound nanoparticles characterized by source-dependent lipid interfaces and associated molecular components, including small RNAs, proteins, polysaccharides, and plant-derived metabolites. We evaluate selected PELNs within a provisional “candidate nano-prebiotic” framework that allows particle- or component-mediated mechanisms to coexist with microbial utilization of fermentable components without implying equivalence to consensus-defined prebiotics. Current evidence remains heterogeneous and predominantly preclinical, with major uncertainties regarding particle identity, component-specific causality, in vivo fate, microbiota dependence, dose plausibility, and mechanistic generalizability. We further define conceptual boundaries among PELNs, conventional prebiotics, and engineered nano-prebiotics and identify priorities for standardization, causal testing, food-relevant exposure assessment, safety evaluation, and mechanistically informed human studies.

The potential role of dietary polyphenols in the management of gastrointestinal issues in autism spectrum disorder.Review Critical reviews in food science and nutrition  |  2026-10-29

Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) is a complex neurodevelopmental condition, frequently comorbid with heterogeneous gastrointestinal (GI) pathologies, including Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS), Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD), and functional motility disorders. Emerging evidence implicates the microbiota-gut-brain axis in the pathogenesis of these GI anomalies. Dietary polyphenols, characterized by potent antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and neuromodulatory properties, represent a promising candidate approach for exploring the management of these comorbid conditions. This review critically examines the proposed molecular mechanisms by which specific phenolic compounds (e.g., resveratrol, quercetin and curcumin) may mitigate GI symptoms in ASD. Mechanistically, preclinical evidence suggests that polyphenols may help restore intestinal barrier integrity by upregulating tight junction proteins, suppress mucosal inflammation via the downregulation of Nuclear Factor Kappa B (NF-κB)/NOD-like Receptor Family Pyrin Domain Containing 3(NLRP3) pathways, and regulate gut motility through enteric neurotransmitter modulation. However, direct clinical efficacy in individuals with ASD remains largely unproven. Future clinical directions are proposed, focusing on the incorporation of low-fermentable oligosaccharides, disaccharides, monosaccharides, and polyols (FODMAPs) natural food matrices and the development of controlled food fortification strategies over isolated high-dose supplementation. This work provides a theoretical foundation and a concise roadmap for advancing polyphenol-based dietary interventions in the management of GI pathophysiology in ASD.

Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) refers to a chronic and recurrent gastrointestinal disorder, placing a lifelong medical burden on millions of individuals globally. As commensal microorganisms residing in the human intestinal tract, probiotics are capable of safeguarding the intestinal barrier, suppressing inflammatory reactions, and sustaining the balance of intestinal microbiota, with no accompanying adverse effects. Accordingly, probiotics have gained attention as a safe therapeutic approach and a major research focus for IBD treatment. In this study, the effects of Bifidobacterium animalis subsp. lactis ProSci-246 (ProSci-246) were investigated in mice with dextran sulfate sodium (DSS)-induced colitis. These findings provide a scientific basis and novel perspective for the potential application of ProSci-246 as an alternative probiotic for IBD prevention and treatment.

Differential efficacy of interleukin-23 pathway inhibitors in ulcerative colitis according to disease extent: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized trials. European journal of gastroenterology & hepatology  |  2026-10-27

Interleukin-23 (IL-23) pathway inhibitors have demonstrated efficacy for moderate-to-severe ulcerative colitis. However, whether treatment response differs according to baseline disease extent remains unclear. We searched MEDLINE, EMBASE, and CENTRAL from January 2015 through August 2026 for randomized placebo-controlled trials evaluating IL-23 pathway inhibitors in ulcerative colitis. Clinical remission during induction and maintenance therapy was pooled using Mantel-Haenszel fixed-effect models for limited versus extensive disease. Treatment-by-disease-extent interaction was evaluated using within-trial ratios of risk ratios (RRRs). Five randomized placebo-controlled trial programs were included, four contributed extent-stratified induction data, and three contributed maintenance data. During induction, IL-23 inhibitors increased clinical remission in limited disease [risk ratio: 2.58, 95% confidence interval (CI): 1.94-3.43] and extensive disease (risk ratio: 2.25, 95% CI: 1.62-3.13), with no significant interaction (RRR: 0.80, 95% CI: 0.51-1.24, P = 0.31). During maintenance, treatment was effective in limited disease (risk ratio: 1.56, 95% CI: 1.28-1.89) and extensive disease (risk ratio: 3.42, 95% CI: 2.45-4.77), with significant interaction (RRR: 2.16, 95% CI: 1.47-3.19, P < 0.001). Sensitivity analyses restricted to selective IL-23p19 inhibitors yielded consistent results. IL-23 pathway inhibition is effective across disease extents in ulcerative colitis. Disease extent did not modify treatment efficacy during induction, whereas a greater relative treatment effect was observed in extensive disease during maintenance.

Plant Polyphenol-Polysaccharide Complexes for Inflammatory Bowel Disease: Molecular Interactions, Nano-Delivery Design, and Translational Evidence. Nutrition reviews  |  2026-10-27

Plant polyphenol-polysaccharide complexes (PPCs) combine polyphenol bioactivity with the protective, mucoadhesive, and microbiota-accessible properties of polysaccharides. However, the literature often groups chemically distinct polyphenols together, extrapolates structure-function relationships across nonequivalent carriers, and interprets concurrent microbiota or short-chain fatty acid changes as causal mechanisms. This review critically evaluated how molecular interactions and measurable structural attributes of PPCs influence gastrointestinal stability, colon-oriented release, biological effects relevant to inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), and translational readiness. Targeted searches of PubMed, Web of Science Core Collection, Scopus, Embase, and the Cochrane Library were completed through July 20, 2026. Peer-reviewed original studies and relevant reviews published in English were considered. Evidence was classified as direct PPC evidence, PPC-relevant formulation evidence, or non-PPC mechanistic and engineering evidence. Information on carrier chemistry, payload, structural attributes, release behavior, biological outcomes, and translational considerations was extracted. Quantitative findings were compared only when carrier chemistry, payload, test conditions, and outcomes were sufficiently aligned; heterogeneous data were not pooled. Non-covalent assembly and covalent conjugation can alter molecular organization, physicochemical stability, and release, but no universal relationship between degree of substitution and release is supported. Epigallocatechin-3-gallate, quercetin, and anthocyanins require compound-specific carrier selection. PPC and PPC-relevant interventions are associated with changes in NF-κB-related signaling, epithelial-barrier markers, microbiota composition, and individual short-chain fatty acids, whereas receptor- or microbiota-mediated causality remains largely unverified. Responsive platforms also differ in stability, release control, scale-up burden, and clinical readiness. PPC-based nano-delivery is a promising preclinical approach, but translation requires matched studies linking structure, properties, and release, mediator-level mechanistic validation, standardized characterization, reproducible manufacturing, formulation-specific safety assessment, and comparison with established IBD therapies.

Baseline microbial ecology encodes probiotic responsiveness through distinct metabolic and network configurations.★ Cell reports  |  2026-10-26

Probiotic efficacy in inflammatory bowel disease varies widely between individuals, and the sources of that variability remain poorly understood. D-lactate is a microbiota-derived metabolite that accumulates during intestinal inflammation and is associated with epithelial barrier dysfunction, which motivated our use of a consortium selected to contribute none of it. Here we show that its benefit is not an intrinsic property of the administered strains but is determined by the pre-existing ecological state of the recipient microbiome. Across three independent mouse resources, preventive administration reduced disease severity and histopathological damage only where baseline communities carried higher diversity and more complex co-occurrence architecture; in those animals the consortium preserved community structure and metabolic output, restoring disease-disrupted metabolites, and selectively raising butyrate and propionate. Sustained exogenous D-lactate did not abolish protection, placing D-lactate as a tractable readout of ecological restoration rather than its effector. Responsiveness is encoded in the baseline microbiota and predictable before intervention, establishing probiotic efficacy as a stratifiable property of the host microbiome.

Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) is a chronic intestinal disorder closely linked to gut microbiota dysbiosis and metabolic disturbances. This study investigated the alleviating effects and mechanisms of Bifidobacterium animalis subsp. lactis C-2 (C-2) on dextran sulfate sodium (DSS)-induced ulcerative colitis (UC) in mice. The results demonstrated that C-2 significantly ameliorated pathological phenotypes in DSS-induced UC mice, including reduced weight loss, lowered disease activity index (DAI) scores, restored colon length, and diminished inflammatory damage of colonic tissue. Meanwhile, C-2 effectively repaired the intestinal barrier by enhancing the expression of tight junction proteins (Claudin-1, Occludin, ZO-1) and MUC2, thereby reducing lipopolysaccharide in serum and rebalancing pro-inflammatory (TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β) and anti-inflammatory (IL-10) cytokines in the colon and serum. Moreover, C-2 intervention restored short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs) levels. In summary, C-2 alleviates UC in mice through synergistic mechanisms including optimizing gut microbiota composition, boosting SCFA production, and enhancing tryptophan-derived metabolites. Collectively, these findings support the potential of C-2 as a candidate probiotic intervention strategy for IBD.

Epidemiological Evidence, Underlying Mechanisms, and Public Health Implications of Ultra-Processed Food Consumption in Inflammatory Bowel Disease.Review Journal of visualized experiments : JoVE  |  2026-10-23

The overall aim of this narrative review is to synthesize epidemiological evidence linking ultra-processed food (UPF) consumption to the onset and progression of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), examine the proposed pathophysiological mechanisms, and evaluate contemporary clinical nutritional strategies. Epidemiological data demonstrates a robust, dose-response association between high UPF intake and an increased risk of incident IBD, predominantly Crohn’s disease (CD). Furthermore, in diagnosed patients, UPF consumption correlates with elevated clinical activity, inflammatory relapses, and adverse clinical outcomes. Experimental and observational findings suggest four potential pathways linking UPF-related exposures to altered gut immune homeostasis. First, UPF-rich dietary patterns may be associated with microbiota dysbiosis and reduced short-chain fatty acid (SCFA) availability. Second, experimental studies suggest that selected emulsifiers can alter intestinal mucus organization and epithelial tight-junction integrity. Third, increased intestinal permeability may facilitate exposure to luminal bacterial products and downstream inflammatory signaling through Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4) and nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-κB). Fourth, advanced glycation end products (AGEs) may contribute to intestinal inflammatory signaling and epithelial stress, although their proposed effects on energy sensing and autophagy in IBD require further validation. Current clinical guidance supports healthy dietary patterns with limited UPF intake and individualized nutritional care, while evidence that UPF restriction alone reduces IBD flares remains insufficient. The review concludes that mitigating the clinical burden of IBD requires integrating personalized nutritional interventions managed by specialized dietitians, while highlighting the necessity for further research into specific food additives and precision nutrition strategies.

Evidence suggests that propolis is a promising candidate for the treatment of ulcerative colitis due to its antioxidant properties and ability to modulate immune responses and the gut microbiota. This review aimed to analyze studies on the efficacy of propolis and its main active compounds in ulcerative colitis, highlighting mechanisms of action, safety and limitations. A literature search was conducted in the PubMed Central, ScienceDirect, and Scopus databases using the keywords “propolis” AND “colitis”. Original articles presenting in vivo analyses, published in English up to June 2025, were included. Propolis extracts improved clinical and histopathological parameters, particularly by reducing pro-inflammatory cytokine expression, inflammatory cell infiltration, and restoring epithelial barrier integrity. These effects mainly involve modulation of inflammatory signaling pathways, particularly suppression of NF-κB activation, resulting in reduced expression of inflammatory mediators, including TNF-α, IL-1β, and iNOS. Propolis also reduced TLR4 expression, potentially contributing to the control of the exacerbated immune response characteristic of ulcerative colitis. These findings support the potential of propolis as an adjuvant agent in disease management; however, its clinical efficacy in humans remains to be validated in well-controlled and standardized studies.

Genetic variants shape single-cell chromatin accessibility and disease risk. Nature  |  2026-10-28

Understanding how genetic variation shapes gene regulation at single-cell resolution is crucial for elucidating complex disease mechanisms1, but has been limited by the lack of large-scale single-cell multi-omics data2-4. Here we present single-cell assay for transposase-accessible chromatin with sequencing of 3.5 million peripheral blood mononuclear cells from 1,042 donors with matched whole-genome sequencing. Across 28 immune cell types, we tested 440,996 chromatin accessibility peaks and identified 243,225 chromatin accessibility quantitative trait loci (caQTLs) at 100,483 peaks, 60% of them cell-type specific. Rare variant analysis identified 38,161 associated peaks, with predicted transcription factor-binding disruption implicated at individual loci. Colocalization with expression QTLs (eQTLs) from single-cell RNA sequencing data (5.4 million peripheral blood mononuclear cells)5 defined 28,834 candidate cis-regulatory elements. Across genome-wide association studies for 16 diseases and 44 blood traits, caQTLs colocalized 4.5-22.6% more signals than eQTLs alone. Cell-type-specific and tissue-specific context, distal effects and multi-causal-variant models raised genome-wide association study-eQTL concordance from approximately 16% to approximately 65%. Cell state further modulates genetic effects, with 27% of over 200,000 caQTL-cell-state interactions lacking a main effect. At the inflammatory bowel disease risk gene ADCY7, accessibility effects varied with genotype and G protein-mediated pathway activity in CD4+ T cells. Together, these results show that genetic variants shape chromatin accessibility in a cell-type-specific and cell-state-specific manner, influencing immune function and disease risk.

Circulating Zonulin in Irritable Bowel Syndrome: A Systematic Review of Conflicting Evidence and the Critical Impact of Assay Specificity.Review Biomedicines  |  2026-10-16

Background: Increased intestinal permeability is a proposed pathophysiological mechanism in irritable bowel syndrome (IBS), and zonulin, a putative regulator of tight junctions, has been investigated as a non-invasive serum biomarker. However, the diagnostic utility of circulating zonulin in IBS remains controversial. Objectives: To systematically review studies comparing serum or plasma zonulin concentrations between adult IBS patients and healthy controls, and to evaluate the influence of assay methodology on reported outcomes. Methods: A systematic literature search of PubMed was performed up to April 2026; the most recent eligible study was published in June 2024. Studies that enrolled adults with IBS diagnosed by Rome criteria, included healthy control groups, and measured zonulin by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) were eligible. Studies lacking a healthy control group, non-original reports, animal studies, and mixed functional gastrointestinal disorder cohorts without extractable IBS data were excluded. Findings were synthesised narratively due to substantial methodological heterogeneity and concerns regarding the validity of commercially available zonulin kits. Risk of bias was assessed using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale for case-control studies; six studies were at low risk and two at intermediate risk. The review is reported in accordance with the PRISMA 2020 statement, and this abstract follows the PRISMA 2020 for Abstracts checklist. Results: Eight case-control studies were included, comprising 421 IBS patients and 428 controls. Of the seven studies contributing to the primary synthesis, two reported significantly elevated zonulin in IBS, one produced opposite results with two different assays applied to the same samples, and four found no significant difference compared with controls. An eighth study, which combined IBS with inflammatory bowel disease and did not report IBS-specific data, was retained as supportive, non-primary evidence. Crucially, the studies employed different ELISA kits (ALPCO, Cusabio, Immundiagnostik, Zellbio) and different units of measurement. Independent validation studies have demonstrated that the most frequently used commercial kits do not recognise the pre-haptoglobin-2 molecule claimed to be zonulin, but instead detect haptoglobin, complement C3, or properdin. The only study employing a monoclonal antibody specific for pre-haptoglobin-2 observed lower, rather than higher, concentrations in IBS. Conclusions: The existing literature based on commercial ELISAs cannot determine whether pre-HP2/zonulin is altered in IBS, and the single genotype-controlled monoclonal assay suggests lower, not higher, concentrations in IBS. The disparate results are largely attributable to the application of ELISA kits that lack analytical specificity for the proposed target. Future research must use analytically validated assays, such as monoclonal antibody-based methods with concomitant genotyping for haptoglobin polymorphisms, before zonulin can be considered a meaningful biomarker in IBS.
