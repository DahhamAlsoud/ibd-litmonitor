Plant Polyphenol-Polysaccharide Complexes for Inflammatory Bowel Disease: Molecular Interactions, Nano-Delivery Design, and Translational Evidence. Nutrition reviews  |  2026-10-27

Plant polyphenol-polysaccharide complexes (PPCs) combine polyphenol bioactivity with the protective, mucoadhesive, and microbiota-accessible properties of polysaccharides. However, the literature often groups chemically distinct polyphenols together, extrapolates structure-function relationships across nonequivalent carriers, and interprets concurrent microbiota or short-chain fatty acid changes as causal mechanisms. This review critically evaluated how molecular interactions and measurable structural attributes of PPCs influence gastrointestinal stability, colon-oriented release, biological effects relevant to inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), and translational readiness. Targeted searches of PubMed, Web of Science Core Collection, Scopus, Embase, and the Cochrane Library were completed through July 20, 2026. Peer-reviewed original studies and relevant reviews published in English were considered. Evidence was classified as direct PPC evidence, PPC-relevant formulation evidence, or non-PPC mechanistic and engineering evidence. Information on carrier chemistry, payload, structural attributes, release behavior, biological outcomes, and translational considerations was extracted. Quantitative findings were compared only when carrier chemistry, payload, test conditions, and outcomes were sufficiently aligned; heterogeneous data were not pooled. Non-covalent assembly and covalent conjugation can alter molecular organization, physicochemical stability, and release, but no universal relationship between degree of substitution and release is supported. Epigallocatechin-3-gallate, quercetin, and anthocyanins require compound-specific carrier selection. PPC and PPC-relevant interventions are associated with changes in NF-κB-related signaling, epithelial-barrier markers, microbiota composition, and individual short-chain fatty acids, whereas receptor- or microbiota-mediated causality remains largely unverified. Responsive platforms also differ in stability, release control, scale-up burden, and clinical readiness. PPC-based nano-delivery is a promising preclinical approach, but translation requires matched studies linking structure, properties, and release, mediator-level mechanistic validation, standardized characterization, reproducible manufacturing, formulation-specific safety assessment, and comparison with established IBD therapies.

Baseline microbial ecology encodes probiotic responsiveness through distinct metabolic and network configurations.★ Cell reports  |  2026-10-26

Probiotic efficacy in inflammatory bowel disease varies widely between individuals, and the sources of that variability remain poorly understood. D-lactate is a microbiota-derived metabolite that accumulates during intestinal inflammation and is associated with epithelial barrier dysfunction, which motivated our use of a consortium selected to contribute none of it. Here we show that its benefit is not an intrinsic property of the administered strains but is determined by the pre-existing ecological state of the recipient microbiome. Across three independent mouse resources, preventive administration reduced disease severity and histopathological damage only where baseline communities carried higher diversity and more complex co-occurrence architecture; in those animals the consortium preserved community structure and metabolic output, restoring disease-disrupted metabolites, and selectively raising butyrate and propionate. Sustained exogenous D-lactate did not abolish protection, placing D-lactate as a tractable readout of ecological restoration rather than its effector. Responsiveness is encoded in the baseline microbiota and predictable before intervention, establishing probiotic efficacy as a stratifiable property of the host microbiome.

A microbiome-reactive TMIGD2hi Th17 subset is spatially enriched at the intestinal epithelium and expanded in inflammatory bowel disease. Immunity  |  2026-10-26

Subsets of intestinal CD4+ T cells directly recognize commensal bacteria and coordinate tolerance versus protective immunity. Our limited understanding of CD4+ T cell specificities and corresponding functional phenotypes has created a barrier to deciphering the mechanisms of mucosal homeostasis and inflammation mediated by the adaptive immune system. To address this, we leveraged an immunodominant Bacteroidetes epitope in SusC to perform single-cell profiling of the SusC-specific and total CD4+ T cell landscapes in intestinal tissues of patients with inflammatory bowel disease (IBD). Together, our results highlight the utility of tracking commensal-specific T cell responses to define specialized subsets of Th17 cells associated with loss of tolerance to the microbiome.

The intestinal microbiome in inflammatory bowel disease and spondyloarthritides.Review Communications medicine  |  2026-10-24

Extraintestinal manifestations (EIMs) of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) can affect any organ system, ranging in presentation, frequency, and severity; the most common EIM is IBD-associated arthritis (IAA), an inflammatory arthritis affecting both axial and peripheral joints. IAA is a form of seronegative spondyloarthritis (SpA), which includes multiple arthritides, and those with IAA are also likelier to have other clinical features observed in those with SpA without IBD, such as increased rates of uveitis, psoriasis, and HLA-B27 positivity. These overlapping features are not limited to clinical disease features; the microbiome has been heavily implicated in the development of both IBD and SpA and is associated with non-SpA EIMs. In this review, we offer an overview of several bacterial taxa and associated metabolites that have been identified as differentially abundant in both IBD and SpA, both in stool and mucosal assessment, and how these taxa are associated with differences in intestinal permeability and immune profiles. We also describe how the overlapping dysbiotic microbiome and associated metabolome in IBD, EIMs of IBD, and SpA may differentially modulate the immune system, thus contributing to the shared underlying etiopathogenesis of these diseases. Further work is needed to characterize causal, mechanistic relationships beyond pure computationally identified associations, and apply these findings towards targeted therapeutic intervention and personalized medicine in IBD.

Bifidobacterium animalis subsp. lactis C-2 Alleviates DSS-induced Ulcerative Colitis via the Microbiota-tryptophan Metabolism-AhR Pathway. Probiotics and antimicrobial proteins  |  2026-10-24

Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) is a chronic intestinal disorder closely linked to gut microbiota dysbiosis and metabolic disturbances. This study investigated the alleviating effects and mechanisms of Bifidobacterium animalis subsp. lactis C-2 (C-2) on dextran sulfate sodium (DSS)-induced ulcerative colitis (UC) in mice. The results demonstrated that C-2 significantly ameliorated pathological phenotypes in DSS-induced UC mice, including reduced weight loss, lowered disease activity index (DAI) scores, restored colon length, and diminished inflammatory damage of colonic tissue. Meanwhile, C-2 effectively repaired the intestinal barrier by enhancing the expression of tight junction proteins (Claudin-1, Occludin, ZO-1) and MUC2, thereby reducing lipopolysaccharide in serum and rebalancing pro-inflammatory (TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β) and anti-inflammatory (IL-10) cytokines in the colon and serum. Notably, C-2 reshaped the gut microbiota structure, significantly increasing microbial diversity (Shannon index) and enriching beneficial bacteria such as Bifidobacterium and Prevotellaceae. Metabolomic analysis further revealed that C-2 modulated the gut tryptophan metabolism pathway, influencing the levels of tryptophan-derived indole metabolites including indole-3-propionic acid (IPA) and indole-3-acrylic acid (I3A), which were associated with activation of the aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR)/Cyp1a1 signaling pathway to enhance barrier integrity and suppress inflammation. Moreover, C-2 intervention restored short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs) levels. In summary, C-2 alleviates UC in mice through synergistic mechanisms including optimizing gut microbiota composition, boosting SCFA production, and enhancing tryptophan-derived metabolites. Collectively, these findings support the potential of C-2 as a candidate probiotic intervention strategy for IBD.

Fecal microbiota transplant in the treatment of inflammatory bowel diseases: new perspectives on delivery, mechanism, and clinical positioning.★ Inflammatory bowel diseases  |  2026-10-24

The gut microbiota, a complex and dynamic community of microorganisms, has emerged as a crucial determinant of human health and well-being in recent years. This microbial ecosystem plays a pivotal role in various physiological processes, including nutrient metabolism, immune system regulation, and intestinal homeostasis. Disruptions in the composition and function of the gut microbiota have been implicated in a wide range of diseases, such as Clostridioides difficile infection (CDI) and inflammatory bowel diseases (IBD). The elucidation of the fundamental relationship between human disease and the gut microbiome has positioned fecal microbiota transplantation (FMT) as a transformative strategy in modern clinical practice. However, while FMT is increasingly explored for a diverse range of systemic diseases, its clinical implementation is relatively limited beyond the management of recurrent CDI. This review examines the evolving role of FMT within the therapeutic framework of chronic inflammation, focusing specifically on IBD. We compare the variable clinical outcomes in IBD with the highly successful model of recurrent CDI, highlighting mechanistic and clinical determinants of response in a different disease context. Additionally, we evaluate the progression of delivery strategies, from conventional fecal suspensions to standardized oral capsule formulations, and their implications for efficacy, safety, and scalability. Furthermore, we explore the diversifying clinical scenarios where FMT may be incorporated in the treatment paradigm of IBD, including primary treatment of active inflammation, prevention of relapse, and its place in combination with current immunosuppressive therapies.

Clostridium butyricum: A Promising Probiotic for Inflammatory Bowel Disease through Modulation of Gut Microbiota and Immunity.Review Probiotics and antimicrobial proteins  |  2026-10-24

Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) is a chronic, relapsing intestinal disorder. Its pathogenesis involves genetic susceptibility, intestinal dysbiosis, environmental triggers, and altered immune responses. Clostridium butyricum (C. butyricum) is a butyrate-producing gut symbiont that has been used as a probiotic for decades. Clinical studies suggest potential benefits in several conditions, including IBD. Here, we review the current evidence on the probiotic mechanisms of C. butyricum in IBD, emphasizing strain-specific effects and distinguishing well-established findings from preliminary ones. The mechanisms discussed include modulation of gut microbiota, improvement of intestinal barrier function, immune regulation, direct antimicrobial activity in preclinical models, and attenuation of oxidative stress. We also highlight unresolved issues, such as strain heterogeneity, limited clinical data, safety concerns in immunocompromised individuals, and the potential role of host genetic factors. Future work should move beyond general probiotic claims and focus on strain-specific, mechanistically defined applications, including genotype-stratified trials to identify patients most likely to benefit.

Baicalin (BA), a bioactive flavonoid, and levofloxacin hydrochloride (LH), a fluoroquinolone antibiotic, both hold potential for ulcerative colitis (UC) treatment, yet the mechanistic differences in their regulation of gut microbiota and metabolome remain elusive. This study aimed to systematically compare their therapeutic efficacy and underlying mechanisms in a dextran sulfate sodium-induced murine colitis model, with a focus on gut microbial and metabolic modulation. Mice were randomized into Control, model, BA-treated, and LH-treated groups. Disease severity was assessed via body weight changes, disease activity index, colon length, and histopathological analysis. Western blot and enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay were employed to detect NF-κB pathway activation and pro-inflammatory cytokine levels, while 16S rRNA sequencing and metabolomics profiled gut microbiota and metabolites. Both agents markedly ameliorated colitis symptoms, suppressed NF-κB activation, and cytokine expression, with BA showing superior cytokine inhibition. BA enriched beneficial Lactobacillus, regulating isoflavone/alkaloid synthesis; LH elevated Akkermansia abundance and modulated amino acid metabolism. Epithelial Barrier-Injury Transcriptomic Stratification Across Human IBD Mucosal Cohorts. Omics : a journal of integrative biology  |  2026-10-23

Public mucosal transcriptomic datasets provide an opportunity to examine whether experimental epithelial barrier biology is reflected in human inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) endpoints. We constructed a prespecified barrier-injury transcriptomic score from an inositol polyphosphate multikinase-histone deacetylase 3-matrix metalloproteinase (MMP) barrier-biology gene set and epithelial junctional genes, then evaluated it across 10 public IBD mucosal transcriptomic cohorts (2172 scored biopsies) and an independent prospective adult cohort (GSE193677; 1162 participants). Adult cohorts were analyzed for mucosal healing, clinical remission, or treatment response; pediatric and early onset cohorts were analyzed separately. In UNIFI (GSE206285), a higher baseline score was inversely associated with week 8 mucosal healing (odds ratio [OR]: 0.56, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 0.45-0.71, p < 0.001). In GSE193677, the score was associated with IBD compared with control status (OR: 1.71, 95% CI: 1.45-2.02) and with endoscopic and histologic activity. The association was directionally consistent across biopsy-selection analyses and exceeded expression- and variance-matched random-gene estimates (empirical p = 0.034), while MMP and inflammatory comparators were at least as strong. Single-cell data localized the MMP module predominantly to stromal cells. These findings support retrospective transcriptomic stratification of mucosal injury states and define a barrier biology-motivated bulk mucosal readout shaped by stromal inflammatory remodeling in human tissue.

Epidemiological Evidence, Underlying Mechanisms, and Public Health Implications of Ultra-Processed Food Consumption in Inflammatory Bowel Disease.Review Journal of visualized experiments : JoVE  |  2026-10-23

The overall aim of this narrative review is to synthesize epidemiological evidence linking ultra-processed food (UPF) consumption to the onset and progression of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), examine the proposed pathophysiological mechanisms, and evaluate contemporary clinical nutritional strategies. Epidemiological data demonstrates a robust, dose-response association between high UPF intake and an increased risk of incident IBD, predominantly Crohn’s disease (CD). Furthermore, in diagnosed patients, UPF consumption correlates with elevated clinical activity, inflammatory relapses, and adverse clinical outcomes. Experimental and observational findings suggest four potential pathways linking UPF-related exposures to altered gut immune homeostasis. First, UPF-rich dietary patterns may be associated with microbiota dysbiosis and reduced short-chain fatty acid (SCFA) availability. Second, experimental studies suggest that selected emulsifiers can alter intestinal mucus organization and epithelial tight-junction integrity. Third, increased intestinal permeability may facilitate exposure to luminal bacterial products and downstream inflammatory signaling through Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4) and nuclear factor-kappa B (NF-κB). Fourth, advanced glycation end products (AGEs) may contribute to intestinal inflammatory signaling and epithelial stress, although their proposed effects on energy sensing and autophagy in IBD require further validation. Current clinical guidance supports healthy dietary patterns with limited UPF intake and individualized nutritional care, while evidence that UPF restriction alone reduces IBD flares remains insufficient. The review concludes that mitigating the clinical burden of IBD requires integrating personalized nutritional interventions managed by specialized dietitians, while highlighting the necessity for further research into specific food additives and precision nutrition strategies.

IL-10-secreting engineered macrophages alleviate DSS-induced colitis with macrophage phenotypic modulation and gut microbiota remodeling in mice. Bioengineering & translational medicine  |  2026-10-22

Ulcerative colitis (UC) is a chronic recurrent inflammatory bowel disease characterized by abdominal pain, hematochezia, and weight loss. Although remarkable progress has been made toward understanding the pathogenesis of UC, finding more effective treatments for this disease remains a challenge. Interleukin-10 (IL-10) possesses potent anti-inflammatory properties, yet its therapeutic application in UC has failed clinically due to poor targeting and a short half-life. In this study, we developed IL-10-secreting engineered macrophages (IL-10M) based on RAW264.7 cells and evaluated their therapeutic effects in a dextran sulfate sodium-induced colitis model. Intraperitoneal administration of IL-10M alleviated body weight loss, disease activity, colon shortening, and histological injury. IL-10M also reduced mucosal damage and improved tight junction-associated epithelial barrier markers. Flow cytometry further detected engineered macrophage-associated GFP signals in colonic tissues and revealed an early increase in CD206+ endogenous colonic macrophages. Microbiome profiling showed treatment-associated compositional changes, including alterations in Bacteroides acidifaciens and Mucispirillum schaedleri, indicating microbiota remodeling after engineered macrophage treatment. Short-term safety assessment did not reveal overt hematological abnormalities or major histological injury in liver and spleen. Overall, these findings suggest that IL-10-secreting engineered macrophages may represent a promising macrophage-based strategy for mitigating intestinal inflammation in experimental colitis.

Integrating complementary datasets for constructing microbiome-metabolome-disease pathways. Bioinformatics (Oxford, England)  |  2026-10-20

Microbiome-metabolome-disease pathways play a central role in host health, but studying them is limited by the data available. Large clinical microbiome cohorts often lack metabolomic measurements, while multi-omics studies with paired profiles are typically small or incompletely phenotyped. Combining such complementary datasets is challenging: the metabolite is not measured in the clinical cohort, microbiome data are high-dimensional and compositional, and log-ratio analyses depend on an arbitrarily chosen reference taxon. We propose a framework that integrates a large target cohort that has microbiome and outcome data with a smaller external study that has paired microbiome-metabolome profiles. A high-dimensional structural equation model jointly links the microbiome, the metabolome and the disease outcome. It allows for unmeasured confounding and groups microbes into functional categories. We address compositionality with the additive log-ratio (ALR) transformation, estimate parameters by penalized two-stage least squares, and test metabolite-disease associations with a regularized permutation test. A novel multiple-reference procedure treats each microbe in turn as the ALR reference. It combines the resulting p-values with the Cauchy combination test and classifies microbes by stability selection, which makes inference robust to the choice of reference. In simulations, the method controls the type I error rate, and its power increases with both target and external sample sizes. An application to inflammatory bowel disease, integrating the FRAN and iHMP cohorts, reveals biologically interpretable microbiome-metabolome-disease links. The method is implemented in the R package MMDP, freely available at https://github.com/lei-fang-stat/MMDP. Supplementary data are available at Bioinformatics online.

Gut Microbiota-Driven Neuroimmune Crosstalk in Ulcerative Colitis: From Mechanisms to Therapeutic Implications.Review Biomedicines  |  2026-10-16

Ulcerative colitis (UC) is a chronic inflammatory disease in which epithelial barrier injury, microbial perturbation, dysregulated mucosal immunity, and altered neural signaling converge. Although dysbiosis is frequently reported in UC, taxonomic shifts alone do not explain how microbial signals are translated into mucosal inflammation, urgency, pain, relapse, or heterogeneous therapeutic responses. This review develops a focused framework of gut microbiota -driven neuroimmune crosstalk, defined as bidirectional communication through which microbial communities and their metabolites regulate epithelial, neuronal, glial, enteroendocrine, and immune compartments, while neural and immune signals reshape barrier function and microbial ecology. We synthesize evidence on short-chain fatty acids, tryptophan-derived indoles, serotonin signaling, bile acids, microbe-associated molecular patterns, vagal anti-inflammatory reflexes, enteric neurons, sensory neurons, enteric glia, macrophages, dendritic cells, T cells, innate lymphoid cells, mast cells, and neutrophils. Therapeutic opportunities include diet, prebiotics, probiotics, postbiotics, fecal microbiota transplantation, metabolite-based interventions, vagus nerve stimulation, neuropeptide or serotonin pathway modulation, and combination strategies with established anti-inflammatory therapies. UC-specific evidence is emphasized where available, with explicit distinction between human UC findings, broader inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) evidence, experimental colitis models, and general mechanistic studies. A microbiota-neuroimmune framework may help move UC research beyond descriptive dysbiosis toward mechanism-guided precision therapy; however, most causal neuroimmune pathways still require validation in well-phenotyped human UC cohorts, and the proposed endotypes in this review are hypothesis-generating and require prospective validation.

Multidisciplinary care should be regarded as a core organizational principle of modern IBD management. Realizing its full potential will require sustained investment, workforce planning, and prospective research to ensure equitable, high-quality, and sustainable care across diverse healthcare settings.
